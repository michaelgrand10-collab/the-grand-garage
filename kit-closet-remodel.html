What you're building

Three ways to remodel a closet. Good refreshes a standard reach-in with an adjustable wire system, a shoe rack, slim hangers and rechargeable motion lights. Better gives the reach-in a full makeover with a laminate tower that has two drawers, three expandable rods and top shelves. Pro builds out a walk-in: two towers in an L, a wall-anchored four-drawer unit, angled shoe shelves, a barn door, a mirror, valet hooks and a tie and belt rack.

Size check before you buy. A reach-in needs about 24 in. of depth for hangers. A walk-in needs that 24 in. for each wall of hanging clothes plus a clear aisle of about 36 in., so roughly 5 ft deep for one hanging wall and about 7 ft wide to hang on both sides. Each kit system lists the closet widths it fits: Good is a 4 to 6 ft wall, Better a 6 to 9 ft wall, and Pro adds a second tower for a 5 to 10 ft side wall.

The kit covers the closet systems, tools, patch and paint supplies, lighting and hardware. If a walk-in needs a wall moved, a new doorway framed or hardwired lights, that work is listed under buy locally / hire a pro below.

What's NOT on Amazon

Buy these locally, or check with your town first:

  • Interior paint and primer for the closet walls: buy locally so you can match colors (Better and Pro).
  • Walk-in conversions only, hire a pro: moving or removing a wall, framing a new or wider doorway and a header, and permits. Have a contractor confirm whether any wall is load-bearing before it comes out.
  • Framing lumber and drywall for any new walls: 2x4 studs and plates, header lumber, drywall sheets, joint compound, drywall tape, corner bead and screws.
  • Electrical, hire a licensed electrician: any hardwired closet light, switch or outlet. The kit's lights are rechargeable, so they need no wiring.
  • Trim to finish a new opening: door casing and baseboard to match your house.
  • Pro barn door: the door slab (36 to 40 in. wide, 1-3/8 or 1-3/4 in. thick per the track kit) and a 1x6 header board if the studs don't line up with the track holes.
  • Clothes you're done with: a local donation drop-off.

Project overview

  1. Plan and measure. Measure the width, depth and ceiling height of the closet. A reach-in needs about 24 in. of depth for hanging clothes. For a walk-in, plan 24 in. of depth for each wall of hanging clothes plus a clear aisle of about 36 in. Plan double-hang sections (ClosetMaid's typical shelf heights are about 84 and 42 in.) and long-hang sections (about 70 in.), and check your wall width against the range each closet system fits. Kit items: Komelon SL2825 25 ft Self-Lock Tape Measure; Nicpro Mechanical Carpenter Pencils (set with case).
  2. Empty it and remove the old shelf and rod. Take everything out and sort out what you no longer wear. Score the paint along the old shelf cleats, unscrew the rod brackets, pry off the rod, shelf and cleats, and pull any nails. Kit items: DEWALT 20V MAX Drill/Driver Kit with 2 Batteries and Charger (DCD771C2); DEWALT 45-Piece Screwdriver Bit Set (DW2166); IRWIN 16 oz Fiberglass Claw Hammer; STEELHEAD 15 in. Flat Pry Bar with Nail Puller; WORKPRO All-Metal Retractable Utility Knife; DEWALT DPG54 Safety Glasses (ANSI Z87.1+).
  3. Open it up (walk-in conversions only). If the walk-in needs more room than the closet has, moving or removing a wall, framing a new doorway and adding drywall is structural work. Have a contractor confirm whether the wall is load-bearing and pull any permits. Hardwired lights or new outlets need a licensed electrician. This kit uses rechargeable lights, so no wiring is needed.
  4. Patch and paint. Fill the old screw and nail holes with spackling, let it dry, and sand it smooth. For Better and Pro, tape off the trim, lay the drop cloth and paint the walls before anything goes up (paint is a buy-locally item). In Good, painting is optional. Kit items: DEWALT DPG54 Safety Glasses (ANSI Z87.1+); DAP DryDex Dry Time Indicator Spackling, 32 oz; WORKPRO 4-Piece Stainless Steel Putty Knife Set; 3M Drywall Sanding Sponge, Fine/Medium; Bates 19-Piece Paint Roller Kit (tray, 9 in. and 4 in. rollers, brushes, tape, drop cloth).
  5. Find the studs and mark level lines. Locate and mark every stud across the walls the system will hang on. Draw level lines at the track or tower heights. In a walk-in, use the laser to carry one level line around both walls of the L. Better and Pro: assemble each tower on the floor (cam locks, bottom nuts and gusset nails), level the feet and fasten the L-brackets to the wall with the supplied screws and anchors. Pro: set the second tower on the side wall and screw the drawer unit's tip-over restraint into a stud. Safety: wear safety glasses when prying, drilling and cutting rods, and use a stable stepladder for the top shelf. Fasten every tower and drawer unit to the wall as ClosetMaid requires, since an unanchored unit can tip, and don't attach anything to the sides of the towers. Check for wires and pipes before you drill. Moving or removing a wall, framing a doorway and adding hardwired lights or outlets are jobs for a licensed contractor and electrician with the right permits. Never remove a wall until a pro confirms it isn't load-bearing.
